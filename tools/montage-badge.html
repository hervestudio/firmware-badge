Assembly & soldering guide

Pin-by-pin wiring and the full assembly order, from the bare shell to a closed, flashed badge. Written for the Medium (66 × 134) shell — identical for Short/Long, only the battery location and model change.

ESP32-S3 DevKitC N16R8 GC9B72 screen 2.1″ 360×360 SPI TP4056 + 5 V USB-C boost LiPo 505060 · 2000 mAh 3 × 6×6×5 switch

0 · Parts & tools

AWG 28-30 silicone wire — lengths PER RUN (lesson from the build: a uniform 10-12 cm everywhere = impossible to close!): screen→ESP 5-6 cm · tab→ESP 7-8 cm · TP OUT→ESP 6-7 cm · battery→TP 5 cm · buttons→tab 3-4 cm. The shell closes “like a book”, hinge on the wire pass-through window side; NOTHING runs over the ESP; group the wires by destination (kapton every 3 cm). 4 × 100 kΩ resistors, 4 × M2 brass inserts, 4 × countersunk M2×12 screws (12 mm needed since the front face was thickened to 8.75 — M2×10 only bite ~1.2 mm into the insert now), Ø3 × 29.4 mm steel rod (strap), thin double-sided tape (battery), cyanoacrylate glue (caps). Tools: iron + fine tip, solder, Ø1.5-2 heatshrink, wire strippers, craft knife (membranes), insert-setting iron.

1 · Wiring — overview

USB-C ──▶ TP4056 ──B+/B−──▶ LiPo battery (505060) │ └──OUT+ ──▶ DevKit 5V pin OUT− ──▶ GND │ └─(board regulator)─▶ 3V3 ─▶ screen VCC

⚠ Never do this

• Never plug anything into the DevKit's native USB port: GPIO 19/20 (D−/D+) are used as buttons. All flashing goes through Wi-Fi OTA.

• Never connect B+ straight to a GPIO: 4.2 V > the 3.6 V absolute maximum. Always go through the 100 k / 100 k resistor divider.

• Mind the battery polarity at the TP4056 (B+ / B−) — reversing it destroys the protection circuit.

Available wire colors: red black yellow green blue. Where a color is used twice, mark it with a black felt-tip ring near each end (shown dashed on the diagrams). Screen: 10-wire rainbow ribbon cable, 1.27 pitch (QWORK), soldered as one piece — every signal has its own color, no ring needed (see Fig. 2).

GC9B72 screen (4-wire SPI) — 10-wire ribbon cable

Solder the ribbon whole and in the module's pad order (it stays flat, no crossovers). Only the white (SDO) wire is left in the air — it lands right opposite GPIO 46 (pin left empty): cut it flush. On the ESP side the GPIOs are picked so the ribbon covers header rows 13 to 20, contiguous and without crossing; the brown wire carries on 2 rows further down to GND (skipping 5V), and only the red one climbs to the 3V3 at the top of the header. ⚠ Badges wired before 14/08: old mapping SCLK 12 / MOSI 11 / DC 13 / RST 14 → rework those 4 wires on the ESP side to bring them to the new standard (single firmware for every badge).

Stripboard tab — GND bus + resistor dividers

Cut a 5 strips × 8 holes piece (12.7 × 20.3 mm) from the Rademacher board (hard paper: score along the holes with a craft knife, it snaps cleanly). It drops into the lipped seat at the bottom right of the front shell (a dab of glue, or friction). Solder the 4 resistors with their bodies lying flat (~4 mm tall), bridging adjacent strips — standing upright (~11 mm) they exceed the 5.4 mm available under the back shell (build feedback 2026-08-13). Nothing floats loose inside the case any more.

R1 = R2 = R3 = R4 = 100 kΩ, bridging adjacent strips, then laid flat. (No capacitors: filtering is done in software — validated on the first 2 badges.) The GND strip runs down the middle: everything else surrounds it and never jumps more than one strip. The wires leave through the notches in the sides of the seat.

2 · Assembly order

  1. Prepare the shells

    Punch out the 7 sacrificial membranes on the front shell with a craft knife / thin screwdriver: 3 button counterbores + 4 screw seats. Set the 4 M2 inserts with the iron (~200 °C) into the bosses of the back shell — properly vertical, flush with the surface.

  2. Prepare the wires

    Cut and tin every wire (10-12 cm). Cut the 5 strips × 8 holes stripboard tab, solder the 4 resistors then lay their bodies flat (total height ~4 mm: the case only leaves 5.4 mm), solder the wires (see the dedicated table), then clip/glue it into its seat. Tin the pads of the screen and of the TP4056.

  3. Fit the switches

    Insert each switch from the outside through its Ø8.8 hole in the front shell, pushing it forward into its cradle. Fold the legs backwards: they come out through the slots in the support plates. Solder after insertion: the common GND wire daisy-chains all three, then one signal wire per switch. Route the loom towards the top-left window

  4. Fit the screen

    Offer the screen up to its ring, connector tab pointing down (the notch in the ring is made for it). It rests on the lower stop on both sides. Solder the ribbon (10 wires in pad order, white not wired) and feed it through the windows in the ESP walls.

  5. Install the TP4056

    Slide the board into its pocket at the bottom of the front shell, USB-C connector in the opening on the edge. It sits on the support wall and locks under the two retainers. Solder B+/B−/OUT+/OUT− (access window in the middle, between the retainers).

  6. Fit the battery (back shell)

    Battery lying landscape on its shelf, above the screw bosses, tabs facing up. Stick it down with double-sided tape. Route the wires to the TP4056 through the gap above the battery.

  7. Fit the ESP32 (back shell)

    The DevKit wedges between its two walls, held at the top under the band. Connect in this order: common GND, 5V (from OUT+), screen, buttons, gauge dividers. Check that no wire runs across the parting line.

  8. Test before closing

    See the checklist below — everything can be tested with the case open.

  9. Close the case

    Drop the Ø3 steel rod into its strap cradle (bridging the two half-shells). Bring the shells together while tucking the wires in, drive the 4 × countersunk M2×10. Thread the strap through the slot and around the bar.

  10. Finishing touches

    Glue the 4 screw caps into their counterbores (cyanoacrylate, hollowed square facing out). Glue the 3 button caps onto the plungers (a dot of cyanoacrylate in the sleeve recess). Check that every button clicks.

3 · Checklist before closing

  • Boot: “Three Conf” splash + loader, then Idle Rainbow
  • All 3 buttons respond (next / previous / menu) — long press on center = power off, press again = wake
  • Battery gauge: a plausible % in the menu (not --% if the dividers are wired)
  • Charging: plug in the USB-C → TP4056 LED lit + charging bolt on screen
  • Screen sharp, no abnormal tearing (TE connected)
  • OTA mode: hold “top” at power-on → the badge-threejs network shows up
  • No wire pinched at the parting line, no solder joint touching the ESP shield

4 · Flashing (no cable)

Menu → OTA flash mode (or hold “top” at power-on). Connect to the badge-threejs / threejs2026 Wi-Fi network, then:

pio run -e ota -t upload # IP 192.168.4.1

Leaving flash mode without flashing: long press on the center button.

Quick reminders — battery: 5 mm thick maximum; the speaker photo/menu pairing is set in the firmware (tools/photo2header.py); runtime ~8-10 h with the 505060, recharge ~2 h 30 at 1 A.
